\begin{abstract}
The standard move in multi-agent systems is to weight messages by source
reliability. We argue this answers the wrong question whenever reliability
and communication convention are distinct latent variables. When agents
report through \emph{source-specific conventions}, mappings from internal
readings to messages that can invert the signal or transform it depending
on context, the real difficulty is interpreting the message, and no
reliability weight can fix a misinterpretation. We prove two structural
limitations for the convention class studied here. First, no aggregation
rule restricted to nonnegative source weights can implement the
Bayes-optimal decision when a source uses an inverted convention whose
Bayes weight outweighs the remaining sources' combined contribution (the
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonneg}); higher reliability only makes the
hypothesis easier to satisfy. Second, no rule assigning a single scalar
weight per source, of either sign, can implement the Bayes rule when a
source's effective sign depends on observable context. We complement these
with a consistency result: a Bayesian aggregator that infers each source's
latent convention from a support set concentrates on the
observational-equivalence class of the truth as evidence accumulates, so
its predictive log-odds converge to the oracle's. The lesson is to decode
messages rather than weight them. We introduce a finite-grid Bayesian
latent-convention aggregator and evaluate it on a benchmark of eight
involutive conventions. Bayesian aggregation consistently outperforms the
scalar baselines and is competitive with a trained attention calibrator
given identical support information, with the clearest margins at high
convention stability and in larger groups. In multi-agent consensus it
improves group accuracy by up to 38.2 percentage points over naive
averaging, finishing within 4.0 points of an oracle. All results are from
a synthetic benchmark; we make no claim about multi-agent communication
in general.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{multi-agent systems, source calibration, trust aggregation,
latent-variable inference, collective decision-making}
