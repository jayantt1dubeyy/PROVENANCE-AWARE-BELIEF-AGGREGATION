\section{Limitations}

Messages and truth are binary; the multi-class extension is not worked
out, though nothing in the theorems looks essentially binary. The
convention family is finite and fixed, and the aggregator assumes the
candidate set is known; Experiment~G starts on misspecification but an
adaptive, open-world candidate set is future work. Sources were assumed
conditionally independent given the truth; Experiment~H breaks that
assumption with correlated errors and finds the ranking preserved, but
only one correlation structure was tested. Conventions are static;
drifting codes would need online inference, which we do not attempt.

The core evaluation is synthetic, plus one controlled
LLM-communication benchmark in which the models' private beliefs are
supplied rather than elicited, real-human-judgment studies on
CIFAR-10H and on two CrowdTruth tasks, a controlled-panel
validation, and an adversarial suite (Section~\ref{sec:validation}):
no LLM debate, robot teams,
or sensor fusion, and none of these is a substitute for those. An LLM
debate experiment with emergent conventions---incentive-based personas
rather than instructed rules, agents using their own knowl\-edge---is
designed and coded (\texttt{code/llm\_debate.py}) as immediate future
work. The CIFAR-10H study, moreover, exercises only the
reliability-heterogeneity half of the thesis---its annotators share one
response format, so there is no convention to decode. The CrowdTruth
studies do exercise the convention half---workers' affirmation
behaviour flips across relation families---but through a scalable
log-odds decoder rather than the grid Bayes of the synthetic
benchmark, on one dataset family in one domain (medical text); a
different-domain replication, and a human panel experiment with
induced conventions, remain future work (protocols in the
repository). The adversarial suite also prices the static-convention
assumption directly: under drift the decoder's accuracy degrades
gracefully but stale support inflates its confidence, and under an
abrupt convention switch between support and query every
support-based method fails. The statistics are
exploratory---100 seeds for Experiment~A, 30 for Experiments~B, C, E,
and I, ten for the rest---enough to tell stable gaps from noise, not
enough for confirmatory claims.

A recent deployment datapoint is suggestive of the same failure mode
outside simulation. Anthropic's Sonnet~5.5 system card reports
FrontierCode scores of 52.1\% at \texttt{xhigh} effort and 46.2\% at
\texttt{max} effort~\cite{anthropic2026sonnet55card}; the card
reports the scores without attributing the gap to any mechanism.
Secondary coverage of the release attributes the drop to the
multi-agent code review invoked at maximum effort, whose review
subagents sometimes timed out or made out-of-scope edits. We cite the
datapoint as an analogy --- more reviewers, worse outcome --- not as
evidence for our mechanism: it is not a controlled comparison, and it
does not isolate the noise-not-signal effect our $\rho=0.5$ result
identifies.

The learned baselines were each run in a single, untuned training
configuration, and the LLM benchmark's banked numbers were produced by
a pre-fix harness version (labelled as such in \S6.11); both carry
that scope. On the method: the grid is coarse ($\rho$ in steps of $0.1$, $r$ in steps
of $0.05$), though a resolution sweep moves every headline number by at
most $0.004$ (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}); the priors are flat, and
posterior concentration at small $K$ is moderate. Calibration is
reported (NLL, Brier, ECE) but not corrected: the Bayesian posterior is
overconfident at high convention stability, and we leave recalibration
to future work. The claim is about decoding versus weighting, not optimal
grid design.
