\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Nonnegative source weighting is insufficient for the convention
class studied here.} Theorem~\ref{thm:nonneg} proves it; the ablation in
\S6.4 shows it. Anything that multiplies messages by nonnegative scalars
inherits the failure, no matter how the scalars are estimated.

\textbf{Signed scalars are necessary but not sufficient once conventions
depend on context.} Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar} makes this precise: a
source's Bayes log-odds contribution varies with context at any
stability $\rho > 0$ and its effective sign flips with context once
$\rho > 1/2$; no context-independent scalar can represent either. The signed-to-context-conditioned
gap in the ablation is the empirical fingerprint.

\textbf{Explicit context conditioning recovers much of the lost
performance; Bayesian inference over the latent convention adds a
smaller, decomposable margin.} \textsc{ContextConditioned} reaches
$0.7350$ against Bayes's $0.7590$. A 30-seed plug-in ablation
(\S6.4) decomposes Bayes's $7.2$-point margin over the EM decoder of
the same model: $\approx 4.7$ points come from \textsc{EMDecoder}'s
decision rule discarding the fitted stability $\hat\rho$,
$\approx 1.9$ points from the grid posterior's better convention
recovery than EM's point fit ($0.640$ vs.\ $0.393$ of sources at
$K=10$), and $\approx 0.6$ points from posterior averaging itself.
Theorem~\ref{thm:consistency} shows the posterior concentrates
on the observational-equivalence class of the truth as $K \to \infty$;
Experiment~C traces that trajectory empirically. The diagnostic
\textsc{MetaAttn+Dec}, given privileged decoded features, reaches $0.8240$
against Bayes's $0.7590$ and the oracle's $0.8263$ (paired
$\Delta = -0.0023 \pm 0.0008$ to the oracle)---so the learned models'
bottleneck is representational: decoding, not capacity, accounts for
nearly all of their gap. Three further stress
tests sharpen the claim. First, the stronger learned baselines
(\S6.4): a wider attention decoder (\textsc{DeepAttn}) does no better
than the smaller one, while a self-attention meta-learner
(\textsc{TransformerMeta}) finishes half a point below Bayes
($0.7544$ vs.\ $0.7590$; paired $\Delta = -0.0046$, 95\% CI
$[-0.0070, -0.0022]$, $d_z = -0.71$) --- a small but statistically
detectable deficit under the same paired standard we apply elsewhere,
and with $104{,}834$ parameters to \textsc{MetaAttn}'s $25{,}667$ it
is not capacity-matched to it. Capacity alone does not explain the
gap; with enough expressiveness a learned sequence model can nearly
match the Bayesian decoder, but none of the learned methods we tried
beats it. Second, a discriminative
source${}\times{}$context meta-learner
(\textsc{CtxLogReg}, \S6.4, 10 seeds) with the same support information reaches
only $0.637$, below both \textsc{ContextConditioned} and Bayes:
expressiveness without the right inductive bias does not help at $K=10$.
Third, Experiment~H breaks the model's independence assumption with
correlated source errors and finds Bayes hurt no more than the baselines
or the oracle. With matched support information and modest capacity,
building in the generative structure beats learning it from scratch.

\subsection{Limitations}

The benchmark is synthetic: a controlled binary task, involutive
conventions, uniform priors, deterministic contexts. Real systems have
continuous messages, non-involutive mappings, and structured contexts.
None of our theorems depend on binarity in an essential way. But the
experiments do not test beyond it. Correlated sources are tested in
Experiment~H (no disproportionate harm), but the correlation form
is one convenient choice, not an exhaustive study.

The convention family is finite, and the Bayesian method assumes the
candidate set is known. Open-world systems would need to grow the set
adaptively. Experiment~G probes the incomplete-family case and finds smooth
rather than catastrophic degradation, but a principled open-world extension
is future work.

Posterior concentration is moderate at small $K$ and sharpens with support
size, just as Theorem~\ref{thm:consistency} predicts.

Calibration is a second, weaker axis. The Bayesian posterior grows
overconfident as conventions stabilise (ECE from $0.017$ at $\rho=0.5$ to
$0.084$ at $\rho=1.0$), and the learned calibrator's probabilities degrade
as groups grow (ECE from $0.14$ at $N=2$ to $0.34$ at $N=12$). Neither
method's probabilities should be taken at face value without
recalibration.

\subsection{Design implications}

First, \emph{do not aggregate raw messages across
heterogeneous sources}: averaging messages from agents with different
conventions can do worse than chance. Second, \emph{calibrate before
aggregating}: keep a support set per source and combine calibrated
log-odds rather than raw messages. Reliability heterogeneity is about
\emph{how often} a source is right; convention heterogeneity is about
\emph{how} it maps readings to messages. A source can be perfectly
reliable and completely unreadable. A concrete setting where this bites
is multi-agent deliberation: systems that synthesise judgments from
persona-driven agents, where each role induces a stable mapping from
evidence to expressed message, face exactly the convention problem
studied here, and with no ground truth available to adjudicate
disagreements. Decoding each persona's mapping before aggregating is
what separates a forecast from a muddle; we offer this as motivation.
