\section*{Reproducibility}

{\sloppy
Everything is released: the finite-grid Bayesian aggregator, all neural
baselines, the smoke tests, and a brute-force unit test
(\texttt{code/test\_predictive\_fix.py}) that checks the
posterior-predictive log-odds against explicit enumeration. From the
repository root, \texttt{python code/smoke\_test.py} verifies the core
components in under ten seconds; \texttt{python code/upgrade\_n100.py}
reproduces the n100 runs in two invocations ---
\texttt{--only A --seeds 100} for Experiment~A and
\texttt{--only BCE --seeds 30} for Experiments~B, C, and E --- with
calibration metrics and the stronger baselines, while
\texttt{code/run\_all\_n10.py} reproduces the remaining ten-seed
experiments (a few hours on a CPU-only machine).
\texttt{code/analyze\_n100.py} regenerates the confidence intervals and
paired comparisons. The stability-boundary experiment (Section~6.3) is
\texttt{code/boundary\_experiment.py} (torch-free; 30 seeds over the
$\rho \times K$ grid, $\sim$5 minutes on CPU), with figures from
\texttt{code/make\_figures\_expI.py}. The real-human-judgment study is
\texttt{code/real\_world\_cifar10h.py}; it reads the public CIFAR-10H raw
file (\texttt{cifar10h-raw.csv} from \texttt{jcpeterson/cifar-10h};
point \texttt{CIFAR10H\_CSV} at your copy, or use
\texttt{data/cifar10h-raw.csv} in the repo) and writes
\texttt{results/real\_world\_cifar10h.csv} plus
\texttt{results/real\_world\_cifar10h\_5judg.csv}, running in under a
minute on a CPU. All randomness goes through a deterministic
\texttt{SafeRNG} wrapper around Python's \texttt{random.Random}; PyTorch
seeds are set before every model initialisation. The repository's
\texttt{REPRODUCIBILITY.md} documents the environment, the per-experiment
design, the baseline information budgets, and every correction applied
during independent reproduction---including the posterior-predictive fix
described in Section~\ref{sec:method}, which changed the estimator from a
posterior expectation of log-odds to the exact predictive quantity. The
code and data are provided as supplementary material; a persistent DOI
will be added.

An audit-repair pass (October 2026) added \texttt{code/audit\_fixes/}:
the genuine Dawid--Skene EM now used by
\texttt{code/real\_world\_cifar10h.py} (the earlier released version
computed the label posterior once and never iterated it), the 30-seed
plug-in ablation of \S6.4 (grid-MAP and posterior-mean plug-ins,
convention-recovery rates, the per-source log-odds scalar), the
\textsc{CtxLogReg} support-size sweep, and exact parameter counts for
the neural baselines; outputs are in \texttt{results/audit\_fixes/}.
All CIFAR-10H descriptive statistics were recomputed from the raw
release in the same pass. The LLM benchmark's released harness now
saves raw model outputs and logs parse-failure substitutions; its
banked numbers predate those fixes (see \S6.11). A validation
programme in the same month added \texttt{code/pvi\_real/}: the
controlled-panel study and adversarial suite, the CrowdTruth
RelEx/RelDir pipelines with the cross-fitted calibrated aggregators,
and the deep-analysis scripts; every number in \S6.13 recomputes
from the summaries banked in \texttt{results/pvi\_real/}. In the calibration
outputs, \textsc{NaiveMean} and \textsc{MajorityVote} are both
assigned the vote fraction as their probability; their hard decisions
coincide by the sign argument of \S6.7, so their probabilistic
metrics are identical by construction.
\par}
